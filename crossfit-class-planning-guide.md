# CrossFit Class Planning & Timelining Guide

## Core Directives for Class Generation
When generating a lesson plan for a CrossFit class, the total duration must exactly equal **60 minutes**. The lesson plan must be entirely **coach-led**. The time allocated to the Warm-Up, Skill/Strength work, and Cool-Down must dynamically adjust based on the time domain of the Workout of the Day (WOD).

## Standard 60-Minute Class Structure
Every class must consist of four primary parts: Introduction, Warm-Up, Workout, and Post-Workout. When breaking down the timeline, divide the class into the following sequential blocks:

### A. Whiteboard / Introduction (Approx. 3-5 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Brief the workout, set expectations, and reduce pre-workout anxiety.
- **Key Elements to Include:**
  - Explain the workout format (e.g., AMRAP, For Time, Heavy Day).
  - Define the **intended stimulus** (e.g., time domain, loading parameters, cardiovascular vs. muscular stamina focus).
  - Provide scaling options for various ability levels.

### B. General Warm-Up (Approx. 5-10 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Stimulate physiological functions, increase core temperature, improve blood flow, and move joints through a full range of motion.
- **Key Elements to Include:**
  - Cardiovascular element (e.g., 400m run, 500m row, jump rope).
  - Calisthenic-type movements and dynamic stretching.
  - **Rule:** Do not pre-fatigue the athletes for the main workout.

### C. Specific Warm-Up / Skill Work (Approx. 10-20 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Build, practice, and refine proper mechanics specifically for the movements used in the WOD.
- **Key Elements to Include:**
  - Empty barbell or PVC pipe progressions to assess movement and correct faults.
  - Targeted weakness work or skill practice (e.g., handstand holds, double-under practice).
  - Load building: Give athletes time to add weight to reach their prescribed workout loads.
  - **Group Management Rule:** Keep the class on a coach's cadence (controlling when the group moves) to systematically observe individual movement faults.

### D. Break & Logistics (Approx. 3-5 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Final preparations before the high-intensity phase.
- **Key Elements to Include:**
  - Bathroom break and water.
  - Check room layout, equipment spacing, and safe bailing areas.
  - Final workout re-brief to remind athletes of the range-of-motion standards.

### E. The Workout / WOD (Time varies based on WOD)
- **Purpose:** Execute the prescribed workout with high intensity.
- **Coach's Role:**
  - Enforce **threshold training**: pushing athletes to increase speed and load while maintaining sound mechanics.
  - Actively scale athletes mid-workout if their mechanics degrade or if they cannot meet the intended stimulus (e.g., finishing far outside the target time domain).
  - Ensure equipment is handled safely (e.g., using collars on barbells, safely dropping weights).

### F. Post-Workout / Cool-Down (Approx. 3-10 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Regain homeostasis, facilitate recovery, and build community.
- **Key Elements to Include:**
  - Stretching, foam rolling, or mobility work targeting heavily taxed muscles.
  - Log scores on the whiteboard and celebrate personal records (PRs).
  - Clean up equipment.
  - Optional: Brief skill refinement or an un-fatiguing "finisher".

## Special Programming Constraints

### A. Heavy Days (Strength Sessions)
When the WOD is a "Heavy Day" (e.g., 5-5-5-5-5 Back Squat or 3-3-3-3-3 Clean and Jerk):
- **Rep Scheme:** Program small sets (1 to 5 repetitions).
- **Total Volume:** The total volume of working repetitions must be between **7 and 25 reps**.
- **Rest:** Ensure athletes take **3 to 5 minutes of rest** between heavy working sets to ensure full recovery for maximal loading.
- **Warm-Up:** Use 3 to 5 progressive warm-up sets (lighter weight/higher reps progressing to heavier weight/lower reps) before the working sets begin.
- **Safety:** The coach must explicitly teach how to safely bail from a missed lift and how to spot (if applicable, like in a bench press or back squat).

### B. Scaling Rules (Preserving the Stimulus)
When outputting scaling options, ensure the following categories are addressed:
- **Beginners/Deconditioned:** Prioritize mechanics. Modify intensity by drastically reducing load and reducing volume (time, reps, or distance). Substitute complex movements to preserve the original movement's function and range of motion.
- **Intermediate:** Scale specific challenging movements or loads to allow the athlete to keep moving. Vary the scaling options to prevent plateauing.
- **Advanced:** Generally perform the workout as prescribed (Rx'd). Only scale up (increasing weight/reps) if the athlete possesses elite mechanics and needs a unique stimulus for skill development.
- **Injured:** Prioritize pain-free range of motion. Substitute movements that replicate the basic function without relying on the injured joint/body part.
