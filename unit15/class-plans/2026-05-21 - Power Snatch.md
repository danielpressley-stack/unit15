## Class Overview

Today's class focuses on **Olympic lifting technique and barbell cycling under fatigue**. Athletes will spend significant time building to a heavy complex of 1 power snatch followed immediately by 1 hang power snatch, then transition into a conditioning ladder where rep counts climb by 3 each round (3/3, 6/6, 9/9, etc.). The stimulus is strength-development with an emphasis on maintaining clean mechanics as fatigue sets in and managing a sustainable pace through barbell cycling.

## Whiteboard Brief (0:00–3:00)

**Key Points to Communicate:**
- Today is a **heavy day with conditioning finisher**. We are building to a heavy lift first, then working conditioning and pace management.
- The complex is **1 power snatch + 1 hang power snatch**—this tests both the full lift from the floor and the catch position from the hang.
- After we hit our heavy, we will move into a **9-minute ladder**. Reps climb in 3's: 3 + 3, 6 + 6, 9 + 9, etc.
- **Hang power snatches only** in the ladder; **bar-facing burpees** every round.
- **Stimulus:** Barbell cycling, pace management, lifting under fatigue. The goal is to hit sets of 3 continuously and allow singles or doubles as rep count climbs.
- Scaling is built in: reduce weight so you can always move sets of 3 efficiently.

## Specific Warm-Up & Skill Work (8:00–20:00)

### A. Empty Barbell / PVC Progressions (8:00–14:00 — 6 minutes)

**Objective:** Establish movement patterns before adding load.

**Progression Sequence (2 rounds):**
1. **PVC Overhead Mobility** (2 rounds × 30 sec)
  - Cue: "Grip wide enough that when you press overhead, your elbows lock out and your arms feel strong overhead. Keep your shoulders strong and tight."

2. **Empty Bar Power Snatch** (3–5 reps per round)
   - Coach for the **full movement from the floor**. Watch for:
     - Flat shoulders over the bar at address
     - Explosive hip extension (triple extension: ankles, knees, hips)
     - Quick turnover and catch position (quarter squat, elbows high, chest up)
     - Immediate stand

  - **Key Coaching Cue:** "Power snatch does NOT require a full squat catch. Land in a **quarter squat** with elbows locked and arms tight. Stand it up hard."

3. **Empty Bar Hang Power Snatch** (3–5 reps per round)
   - Start from hang position (bar at mid-thigh)
   - Same crisp mechanics as power snatch—hip explosion and fast turnover
   - **Key Coaching Cue:** "From the hang, drive your elbows and catch high. You have less distance to travel, so the bar speed must be *explosive*."

**Coaching Focus:** Emphasize quick elbows and high catches. Power snatch is about speed under the bar, not depth of squat.

### B. Progressive Load Building (14:00–20:00 — 6 minutes)

**Objective:** Systematically add weight and practice the complex under fatigue-free conditions.

**Structure:**
Athletes will work up to a heavy complex in 5–6 sets. Start light and add small increments (typically 5–10 kg per set for each gender). Each athlete moves at their own pace within the coach's cadence.

**Set Breakdown:**

| Set | Weight | Reps | Rest | Notes |
|-----|--------|------|------|-------|
| 1   | 40% of estimated max | 1 Power Snatch + 1 Hang Power Snatch | 45 sec | Establish rhythm; focus on technique |
| 2   | 55% of estimated max | 1 + 1 | 60 sec | Move with control; bar speed should be fast |
| 3   | 70% of estimated max | 1 + 1 | 90 sec | Loading phase begins; heavier but still crisp |
| 4   | 80% of estimated max | 1 + 1 | 2 min | Building intensity; stay patient with the bar |
| 5   | 90%+ of estimated max | 1 + 1 | 2–2:30 min | **HEAVY SET**—go to RPE 8–9; if form breaks, stop and reset |

**Coach's Role During Load Building:**
- **Call out each set by percentage.** e.g., "Set 1: Light complex at 40%. Building confidence. Let's see crisp catches."
- **Watch for common faults:**
  - Loose catches (elbows dropping, shoulders shrugging)
  - Early arm bending (pulling with arms instead of legs)
  - Rushed transitions between power snatch and hang power snatch
  - Missed reps or bailouts → have athlete take a breath, reset, and retry

- **Positive reinforcement:** Celebrate PRs and crisp reps. "That catch was *tight*. That's the speed we want."
- **If an athlete misses:** Cue them to take a breath, shake out their hands, and re-approach. Offer a light spot/encouragement if needed.

**Time Management:** At the 35-minute mark, athletes should have completed their final heavy set. If someone is running late, remind them to wrap up quickly.

## Break & Logistics (36:00–41:00)

**Objective:** Final preparations for the conditioning phase.

**What Happens:**
- Athletes take a **5-minute break**: water, bathroom, stretch.
- Coach resets the clock and clarifies the **ladder format**:
  - Round 1: 3 hang power snatches + 3 bar-facing burpees
  - Round 2: 6 hang power snatches + 6 bar-facing burpees
  - Round 3: 9 hang power snatches + 9 bar-facing burpees
  - Continue adding 3 reps each round for 9 minutes **total.**
  - **No separate barbell or burpee setup needed**—athletes cycle between the two throughout.

- **Barbell Weight for the Ladder:**
  - Use **30 kg for women** and **40 kg for men** as the starting weight.
  - Athletes who feel strong can go heavier; those who need to focus on mechanics can go lighter.
  - The goal is to **manage sets of 3 continuously.** Sets do NOT need to be unbroken as volume climbs.

- **Range of Motion Standards:**
  - **Hang Power Snatch:** Bar must travel from mid-thigh, athlete must catch in a stable position (quarter squat or deeper), and stand fully upright.
  - **Bar-Facing Burpees:** Hands on the ground, chest + thighs touch the deck, jump or step back, jump or step forward, jump over the barbell.

- **Safety Check:**
  - Confirm all barbells have **collars on**.
  - Verify **clear floor spacing** between barbell stations (at least 1–2 meters).
  - Check for any loose bumper plates or bent bars.

## WOD: 9-Minute Ladder — Hang Power Snatch + Bar-Facing Burpees (41:00–50:00)

### Format

**As many rounds as possible (AMRAP) in 9 minutes:**
- Round 1: 3 hang power snatches (30/40 kg) + 3 bar-facing burpees
- Round 2: 6 hang power snatches + 6 bar-facing burpees
- Round 3: 9 hang power snatches + 9 bar-facing burpees
- Round 4: 12 hang power snatches + 12 bar-facing burpees
- Continue climbing by 3 reps each round until time expires

### Stimulus & Intended Outcome

- **Time Domain:** 9 minutes (moderate conditioning)
- **Focus:** Barbell cycling, pace management, and maintaining technique under fatigue
- **Expected Rounds:** Most athletes will complete rounds 1–3 and start round 4, depending on weight and ability
- **RPE During Wod:** 7–8 out of 10 (working hard, but not an all-out sprint)

### Coach Guidance During the WOD

**Opening 2 Minutes:**
- Athletes should move with **intent but not panic.** Hang power snatches are quick; sets of 3 should feel manageable.
- Cue: "Pace this like you have a plan. Small, consistent sets. The barbell gets lighter as you get tired, but the reps climb—manage your effort."

**Minutes 3–6 (Rounds 2–3 begin):**
- Monitor for **mechanical breakdown.** As reps climb, watch for:
  - Sloppy catches (incomplete lockout, elbows dropping)
  - Shortened range of motion on burpees
  - Athletes rushing instead of cycling

- If mechanics degrade, **actively scale during the workout**: "Drop to 25 kg and keep moving" or "Take singles if needed—let's preserve your form."

**Final 3 Minutes (Final push):**
- Encourage athletes to push the pace if they have it, but **never at the expense of technique.**
- Cue: "You have 3 minutes left. Can you complete one more full round? Let's see it."
- Some athletes may drop out of the ladder and do partial rounds or unbroken sets as fatigue peaks—this is normal and acceptable.

### Scaling Options

| Athlete Level | Barbell Weight | Modifications |
|---------------|---|---|
| **Beginners / Deconditioned** | 15–20 kg (or empty bar) | Reduce reps by half (1.5/3/4.5 ascending). Substitute dumbbell snatches (light load) for barbell snatches if barbell mechanics are rough. Substitute box-step burpees instead of bar-facing burpees. |
| **Intermediate** | 25–30 kg (women) / 35 kg (men) | Perform as written. If barbell feels too heavy by round 3, drop to a lighter barbell. |
| **Advanced** | 30–40 kg (women) / 50–60 kg (men) | Perform as written. Consider increasing weight or adding a final "sprint round" of 15 reps if they're finishing before the 9-minute cap. |
| **Injured (Shoulder/Wrist)** | N/A | Substitute dumbbell/kettlebell snatches or replace snatches entirely with a complementary movement (e.g., thrusters, wall balls). Perform bar-facing burpees as tolerated. If burpees are restricted, substitute a cardio element (row/ski/assault bike calories). |

## Cool-Down & Recovery (50:00–60:00)

**Objective:** Lower heart rate, facilitate recovery, and build community.

**Structure (10 minutes):**

1. **Light Movement** (2–3 minutes)
   - Walk and shake out arms and legs
   - Light shoulder rolls and neck mobility

2. **Mobility Sequence** (5 minutes)
   - **Overhead Shoulder Stretch** (30 sec each side, 1 min both): High plank position, rotate torso, reach arm across and behind head
   - **Child's Pose** (1 min): Deep breath into posterior chain
   - **Quadriceps Stretch** (30 sec each leg): Standing or lying
   - **Hip Flexor Stretch** (30 sec each leg): Low lunge position
   - **Thoracic Rotations** (30 sec each side): Open up the torso

3. **Log Scores & Celebrate** (1–2 minutes)
   - Athletes call out their final round completed (e.g., "Round 3 + 7 reps")
   - Celebrate PRs, improved mechanics, or strong effort
   - Athletes record scores on the whiteboard

4. **Equipment Check** (1 minute)
   - Racks are cleared
   - Barbells racked, collars removed
   - Floor is tidy

## Safety & Layout Reminders

### Barbell Setup
- **Collars are MANDATORY** on all barbells during both the heavy complex and the ladder.
- Verify bumper plate condition; replace any visibly cracked or delaminated plates.
- Use 20 kg Olympic barbells; confirm scale calibration if you suspect bent bars.

### Floor Layout
- **Minimum 2 meters of clearance** between each barbell station. During the ladder, burpees will happen over the barbell, so ensure no athlete is in the landing zone of another.
- **Separate zones:** Designate one area for power snatch work (heavy building phase) and one for the ladder (if space allows). This prevents congestion.
- **Spectator area:** If athletes are resting or waiting, keep them out of the active lifting zones.

### Bailing & Safety Drills
- **Power Snatch Bails:**
  - If the lift fails at the catch, teach athletes to **bail forward** (let the bar fall in front of them).
  - Emphasize: never try to force a failed catch or hold a heavy misses bar overhead.
  - If needed, practice 1–2 intentional bails during the load-building phase to build confidence.

- **Burpee Over Barbell:**
  - Ensure the barbell is stable in the rack or on blocks during setup.
  - Athletes should jump or step over the barbell with control.
  - If an athlete clips the barbell, remind them: land safely, reset, and continue.

### Heart Rate & Fatigue Management
- Some athletes may feel lightheaded or dizzy during the cool-down after an intense effort. Encourage them to **sit down, drink water, and breathe deeply** if needed.
- If any athlete appears to be struggling or in distress, pause the class and check in.

---

## Additional Notes for the Coach

1. **Pacing the Heavy Complex:** This phase should feel **controlled and strong**, not rushed. Athletes have 14 minutes to load up to their heavy set. Use this time to build confidence and watch for technique flaws.

2. **Ladder Pacing:** The stimulus is **barbell cycling and fatigue management**. It's not a sprint. Encourage athletes to settle into a sustainable pace (especially in rounds 1–3) and adjust scaling if needed.

3. **Movement Quality Over Speed:** Emphasize that a crisp, locked-out catch at 30 kg is better than a sloppy catch at 40 kg. Clean mechanics under fatigue is the skill we're developing.

4. **Community Aspect:** Celebrate effort during the cool-down. Lifting is personal, but the class environment should feel encouraging and supportive.
